Signal-to-Proof

A marketing measurement decision system that determines when behavioral signals support correlation, when stronger causal inference is defensible, and what experiment or evidence is required before a business scales an intervention.

It connects data readiness, statistical modeling, identification, counterfactual design, evidence gates, and business action in one claim-safe system.

A public technical demonstrator by Gerry Sun / GRDigital

The governing rule A more sophisticated model is not automatically stronger evidence. Stronger evidence requires stronger identification.
Measurement as a decision system

Correlation and causal inference sit inside a larger business decision architecture.

1. DecisionWhat commitment is leadership considering?
2. EconomicsWhat is the value at risk and cost of being wrong?
3. BehaviorWhat product and UX signals can actually be observed?
4. EvidenceWhat can correlation, controls, and identification support?
5. ActionScale, test, redesign, maintain, or stop.

Cross-disciplinary build
Strategy frames the choice. Economics defines the stakes. Product and UX create the observable signals. Data engineering makes them usable. Statistics measures the relationship. Causal inference determines effect. GTM turns evidence into action.
Core principle

Prediction is not effect.

ML can help identify who is likely to move. Causal inference asks what changed because the system intervened.
